title: "Schedules and tasks"
description: "Switch recurring platform routines on or off, change their schedules, and see what background work is currently running."
---

Two admin pages cover recurring and running work: **Schedules & Tasks** for the platform's recurring routines, and **Tasks** for a live view of background jobs.

In the console's six labelled areas, both pages are one item, **Platform › Schedules and tasks**. A strip of two tabs, **Schedules** and **Tasks**, at the top of either page opens the other. With the six areas turned off (see [Six labelled areas](/admin/overview#six-labelled-areas)), the two pages are separate items in the sidebar and show no tabs.

## Manage schedules

Open **Schedules & Tasks**. It lists every recurring routine the platform runs: the scheduled jobs of the main service (index schedule scan, pipeline schedule scan, artifact retention, token expiry notices) and the workers of the scheduler service (price sync and the retention sweeps). Each row shows the schedule in force, whether the routine is on, when it last ran and its outcome, and when it runs next.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Switch a routine off or on">
    The switch stops a routine from running without deleting anything. A routine that is switched back on waits for its next scheduled time; it does not replay the runs it missed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change its schedule">
    Click the schedule and enter an interval such as `30m` or `6h`, or five cron fields. Each routine has a shortest allowed gap, and a faster schedule is refused with the reason. Leave the field empty to go back to the default that the deployment sets.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the result">
    Changes apply within about a minute, without restarting anything. Every change is recorded in the audit trail with who made it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot id="admin-schedules-table" alt="Schedules & Tasks page listing the platform's recurring routines" />

<Note>
What a routine does is fixed in code and cannot be edited here: only whether it runs, and when. The editing controls need the `configuration.scheduling.schedules.edit` permission; with only the view permission the page is read-only. If the scheduling registry cannot be read, the main service's scheduled jobs pause until it can, rather than running unchecked.
</Note>

## Marketplace snapshot retention

The routine `main.marketplace_retention` runs once a day and pauses in maintenance. It deletes withdrawn Marketplace listing versions, the snapshots, that no project has installed or as a fork origin. It deletes them 30 days after the publisher unpublished them. It also deletes a listing that has no version left. A snapshot that a project holds is never deleted.

The 30 days are the platform's choice. ADR-0051 says snapshots go "after the ADR-0042 retention" and gives no number. The value is a constant in the Main service (`SnapshotRetentionDays`), and you cannot change it on this page. You can switch the routine off or change its schedule here, like any other routine.

## Marketplace signals

The routine `main.marketplace_signals` runs once a day and pauses in maintenance. For each listing that has a hire it writes the active hires, the 30-day retention, the dismissal rate and the upgrade adoption, for the listing and for each version, and it refreshes the ratings from the visible reviews. The Marketplace page and the admin published agents view read them. The project counter of the published agents view is the number of projects with an active hire. A hire counts as dismissed when its agent is deleted from the project. You can switch the routine off or change its schedule here.

## Break-glass elevation reconciler

The routine `main.elevation_reconciler` runs every minute and keeps running in maintenance. It records each break-glass elevation that ran out as ended with the reason `expired` and moves it to review, and it records each elevation request that nobody answered within the request lifetime as `request_expired`, each with its security audit event. It never decides access: an elevation stops granting at its expiry whether or not the routine has run. You can change its schedule here; switched off, the records and their events wait until it runs again. See [Break-glass access](/admin/break-glass#when-an-elevation-or-a-request-runs-out).

## Access notice sender

The routine `main.access_notice_sender` runs every 30 seconds and keeps running in maintenance. It sends the break-glass notices and the notice to owners of tokens with inherited administration access by e-mail, and retries a notice the mail server refused, after a minute at first and at most every hour. With no outbound e-mail configured it sends nothing and the notices wait. You can change its schedule here; switched off, the notices wait until it runs again. See [Who is told](/admin/break-glass#who-is-told).

## See what is running

Open the **Tasks** tab (or, with the six areas off, the separate **Tasks** page) for a live view of background work this platform actually tracks: index runs, agent turns, toolkit tool calls, scheduled occurrences, and evaluation runs.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Filter by kind">
    Narrow the list to one kind of task.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cancel a task">
    The cancel control only appears on rows the server reports as cancellable — a task past that point does not offer it, rather than offering a control that would mostly answer with a conflict.

    Cancelling a task needs `platform.operations.manage` or `runtime.plugins`; reading the list needs `platform.operations.view` or `runtime.plugins`. Both administrator roles hold all three.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot id="admin-tasks-table" alt="Tasks page listing running background work" />

<Note>
There is no start control and no log tail here. Starting a task and reading its output are not modeled by this page — a task's output is its own artifact rather than a live log stream.
</Note>
