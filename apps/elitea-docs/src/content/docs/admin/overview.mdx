The admin console is a separate application at `/admin/app`, distinct from the main Elitea app. It is where an administrator manages the deployment as a whole: users, projects, authentication, the LLM proxy, budgets, branding, and platform-wide configuration.

## Open the admin console

Sign in to Elitea, open the account menu at the bottom of the sidebar, and select **Admin portal**. The item is there only when your account holds an administration permission. You can also go to `/admin/app` directly. The console renders only if your account holds at least one administration-mode permission — otherwise you see an access-denied screen instead of an empty shell. Every individual page still enforces its own permission on the server on every request; the console's own check only decides whether it is worth rendering the navigation at all.

## Six labelled areas

The sidebar lists the console's pages in six labelled areas, in this order. Each area holds at most eight items. In a short window the links scroll, and your account stays at the bottom. When the sidebar is collapsed, the area labels are hidden and a line separates the areas; screen readers still announce each label.

| Area | Items |
| --- | --- |
| **People and access** | Users, Access, Identity providers, SCIM |
| **Projects** | Projects, App Requests |
| **Models and spend** | Providers and models, Models and pricing, Tool prices, Usage, Logs, LLM Governance, Budgets and alerts |
| **Agents and governance** | Agent registry, Connector requests, Delegations awaiting review, Toolkits, Credential inventory, Permission trimming, Kill switch |
| **Security** | Audit Trail, Data retention |
| **Platform** | Configuration, Features, Branding, E-mail, Schedules and tasks, Service Descriptors, Secrets, Client applications |

**Schedules and tasks** is one item for two pages: the recurring schedules and the background tasks. A strip of two tabs at the top of either page links the other, and the item is marked on both. The old addresses `/admin/app/schedules` and `/admin/app/tasks` keep working.

**Access** lists every administration role: what it opens, and whether you may give it to someone (see [Users and roles](/admin/users-and-roles#role-definitions)). The permission matrix, `/admin/app/roles`, is reached from Access by a caller who can read it. **Identity providers**, **SCIM** and **Client applications** are the editors that used to be sections of **Configuration**; their Configuration sections now link to these pages. The LLM gateway tabs that were in **Configuration › LLM Proxy** are the pages of **Models and spend** (see [LLM proxy](/admin/llm-proxy#where-the-pages-were)).

## What a narrow role sees

The areas and items in the sidebar come from the [capability document](#what-the-console-shows-follows-your-roles): an item is listed when one of your administration roles opens it, and an area is listed when it has an item for you. An area with nothing for you is left out. For the built-in roles:

| Role | Areas and items |
| --- | --- |
| Global administrator, Platform administrator | All six areas and all their items |
| LLM manager | Projects (Projects, view only), Models and spend (all seven items; Usage and Logs view only) |
| Project administrator (all projects) | People and access (SCIM), Projects (Projects, App Requests), Models and spend (Budgets and alerts) |
| Security auditor | People and access (Access, view only), Security (Audit Trail) |
| Emergency access | None: its one item, Break-glass, has no page yet |

## Home

The console opens on **Home**, at `/admin/app`. Home has one card for each area your roles open, listing the items you can open there, and a **Waiting for you** section with the queues you decide:

| Queue | Shown when your roles |
| --- | --- |
| App requests awaiting a decision | can decide App Requests |
| Connector requests awaiting a decision | can decide Connector requests |
| Delegations awaiting review | can decide Delegations awaiting review |

Each queue shows how many requests wait in it and links to its page. A queue you can only view is not shown, since nothing in it waits for you. When a count cannot be read, Home says so instead of showing zero.

If your roles open only one area, the console skips Home and opens that area's first item. If they open no area, the console shows a page that says you have no administration access and whom to ask: your deployment's IT support contact, or otherwise the people who manage the deployment. That page has no sidebar and names no administrator.

If you type the address of a page your roles do not open, the console shows an access-denied page for that item instead. It names the item, lists the roles that open it, and says whom to ask: the IT support contact your deployment has set, or otherwise the people who manage the deployment. It never names an administrator. If the roles cannot be read, the page says so rather than claiming that no role opens the item.

The sidebar only decides what to show. The server checks every request, so a control on a page you can open may still be refused.

## View-only pages

A role can open a page to read it without being able to change it. On such a page the console shows a **View only** notice under the title, and the notice names the roles that can change the page. Every control that would change something, such as save, edit, clear or delete, is shown but disabled, and its tooltip says why. Controls that only read, such as search, tabs, paging and the member list, keep working.

The level comes from the same capability document as the sidebar: each item is open to you to read or to change. If the console cannot read the document, it does not turn a page read-only; the server answers each change for itself, as before. If it cannot read the roles that can change the page, the notice says the page is view only without naming a role.

Every page of the console that can change something has this mode; a page with nothing to change, such as **Agent registry** or **Audit Trail**, has no notice. An action whose permission is narrower than its page is checked on its own: on **Data retention**, for example, a role that holds only `legal_hold.manage` can place and release legal holds while the retention policy stays disabled. Some parts of a page have their own level, so you can be able to change one part and only read another:

- Each LLM Proxy tab (providers and models, models and pricing, tool prices, budget alerts).
- The **Identity providers** and **SCIM** pages. On both, the trust controls have a level of their own: adding, deleting, turning on or off a provider and changing its issuer, metadata, certificates, secret or identity attributes, and creating, rotating or deleting a SCIM client, need `identity.sso.trust.manage` or a global administrator. A role with only `identity.sso.settings.manage`, and the platform administrator, see those controls disabled with a notice that says so, and keep the provider's name, scopes and login domains, revoking a SCIM client, and the group bindings.
- The editors that Configuration shows for E-mail (the **E-mail** item, also on its own page) and MCP servers (the **Toolkits** item). Configuration's own **View only** notice sits above its ordinary section form, and each of these editors shows its own.

A control that used to be hidden from a role that cannot use it, such as **New role** on Roles, **Delete** on Users or **Create secret** on Secrets, is now shown disabled. Some actions that look like changes only read, so they keep working on a view-only page: revealing a secret's value, downloading the branding package, showing resumed suspensions on the Kill switch, and listing a toolkit's project exceptions.

While the console reads the capability document, the sidebar shows that it is loading. If the read fails, the sidebar says so and offers **Try again**; it never shows an empty menu for a failed read.

### The six areas on an upgraded deployment

The six areas are controlled by **Admin › Features › Administration console › Six labelled areas** (`admin_console_areas`). A new deployment has it on. On a deployment upgraded from a release that already had administrators, the upgrade turns it off for one release, so administrators keep the navigation they know: two unlabelled groups, with **Roles** among the first, and **Tasks** and **Schedules & Tasks** as two items. In that navigation an item is listed when you hold a permission its page accepts. Turn the setting on when you are ready; a later release removes the old navigation.

## Account menu

Select your name at the bottom of the sidebar, or your picture when the sidebar is collapsed, to open the same account menu as in the Elitea app. It has **Back to Elitea**, which returns you to the app, the version line (the console's version and the server's; select it to copy it) and **Sign out**.

The console has no theme switch of its own. It uses the theme that you choose in the Elitea app, under **Settings › Personalization** or **Appearance** in the account menu, so the console and the app always look the same.

## What each page can and cannot decide

`window`-injected permission flags in the console are presentation only: they decide what the sidebar shows, never what a request is allowed to do. Every read and write is re-authorized on the server against the actual permission that route requires. A typed URL to a page your account cannot use still reaches it — and still gets refused there.

## What the console shows follows your roles

The server states what the console may show in one capability document, `GET /api/v2/admin/console`. It lists the six areas of the console (People and access, Projects, Models and spend, Agents and governance, Security and Platform), the items of each area that your roles open, and for each item whether you can change it (`write`) or only look at it (`read`). It also lists your administration roles, and carries `admin_console_areas`, the setting that turns the six areas on. An item that none of your roles opens is not in the document, and an area with no such item is not either.

The document is computed from the same permission table that the server's checks use, so the console and the server cannot disagree about what a role may do. It names roles, never permissions and never people. If you hold no administration role, the document is empty rather than refused, so the console can tell "you have no access" apart from "the request failed".

`GET /api/v2/admin/console/items/{item_id}` names the roles, built-in and custom, that open one item, so a page you cannot open can tell you which role to ask for. It names no administrator.

The sidebar's six areas are drawn from this document. Like the permission list described above, it only decides what to show: the server still checks every request.
