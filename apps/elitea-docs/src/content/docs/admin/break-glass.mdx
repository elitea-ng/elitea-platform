title: "Break-glass access"
description: "Time-boxed elevations of one browser session: the settings that bound them, how they run out, how they move to review, and the sealed session used when single sign-on is down."
---

Break-glass access gives a person the administration permissions of the `admin` or `super_admin` role for a short time, in one browser session, with a stated reason. An elevation is not a role: it is bound to the session that asked for it, it ends by itself, and every elevation is reviewed after it ends.

<Note>
This release ships the policy, the ledger, its housekeeping and the request and approval routes. No route starts an elevation yet: the start and the end come in a later release, so an approved request grants nothing on its own.
</Note>

## Settings

The **Break-glass** section of **Configuration** holds the policy. Only a holder of `access.breakglass.manage` sees it or changes it. Changing it is a `critical` action: it is refused under an elevation and refused to every personal access token.

| Setting | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Approval required | on | on or off (see below) |
| Online default duration | 60 minutes | 1 to the online cap |
| Online maximum duration (the cap) | 240 minutes | 1 to **480** (the ceiling in code) |
| Sealed default duration | 60 minutes | 1 to the sealed cap |
| Sealed maximum duration (the cap) | 120 minutes | 1 to **240** (the ceiling in code) |
| Request lifetime | 30 minutes | 1 to 1440 |
| Review time, online | 72 hours | 1 to 720 |
| Review time after a sealed sign-in | 24 hours | 1 to 720 |
| Re-authentication window for the start | 15 minutes | 1 to 15 |
| Approval-off delay | 24 hours | 1 to 168 |

A deployment can lower any value. It can raise a cap only up to the ceiling in code: 480 minutes online and 240 sealed. A save above a ceiling, or a default above its cap, is refused with `400` and names the setting.

### Switching approval off

Dual control is the default: a second person with `access.breakglass.approve` approves every request. Switching approval off:

- needs `admin.auth.users.super_admin` from a standing role. A holder of the section without it gets `403`, and still edits the durations. An elevation never counts towards it;
- takes effect after the **approval-off delay** that was in force before the save (24 hours by default), so a save that also lowers the delay does not shorten its own wait. Until then the section shows the state `off_scheduled`, and a request still needs an approver;
- writes a security audit event at once: `admin.access.break_glass_approval` with the reason code `approval_off_requested` (see [the audit event schema](/reference/audit-event-schema)).

Shortening the approval-off delay needs the same standing `super_admin` (`403` otherwise), since a shorter delay weakens the next switch-off as much as the switch-off itself. Lengthening it needs only the section.

Switching approval back on needs only the section, takes effect at once, cancels a pending switch-off, and writes the same event with `approval_on_restored`.

## Requesting and approving

An elevation starts as a request, and a second person answers it. Both are calls of the administration API today; the console pages that make them come in a later release. Only a browser session makes them: a personal access token, a native app session, a run token and a sealed session are refused with `403 elevation_credential_refused` on every route below, whatever they hold.

**Request.** A holder of `access.breakglass.request` through a standing role (the `emergency_access` role, or `admin` and `super_admin`) asks with `POST /api/v2/admin/access/elevations`:

```json
{"reason_code": "incident", "reason": "The model provider is down and the routing must be changed now.", "ticket": "INC-4711", "duration_minutes": 60}
```

- `reason_code` is `incident`, `identity_outage`, `recovery` or `other`, and `reason` is required.
- `ticket` is optional.
- `duration_minutes` is how long the elevation lasts once it starts. Without it, the request takes the **online default duration** of the settings. Above the **online maximum duration** it is refused with `400 elevation_duration_over_cap`.
- One request is open at a time: a second one is refused with `409 elevation_request_pending` while the first waits for an answer, and while an approval of it has not started, for one request lifetime from the approval. With approval off, that is what keeps a person to one request per lifetime. There is no extension: more time is a new request after the elevation ends.

Every holder of `access.breakglass.approve` except the person who asked gets an e-mail with a link to the approval page (see [Who is told](#who-is-told)). The request waits for the **request lifetime** (30 minutes by default).

**Approve or deny.** A holder of `access.breakglass.approve` through a standing role answers with `POST /api/v2/admin/access/elevations/{id}/approve` (no body) or `.../deny` with a reason code the person reads: `{"reason_code": "standing_access_suffices"}`. The denial codes are `not_justified`, `more_information_needed`, `standing_access_suffices` and `other`.

- **The level is the approver's.** An approver who holds `super_admin` through a standing role approves at the `super_admin` level, anyone else at the `admin` level. Nobody can approve `super_admin` without being one.
- **Nobody answers their own request** (`403 elevation_own_request`).
- **The person must still be able to hold it.** An approval is refused with `403 elevation_requester_not_eligible` when, since the request, the person's account was suspended, the person lost `access.breakglass.request`, or the session the request was made in ended. A denial is still accepted.
- **Nobody under an elevation answers** (`403 elevation_approver_elevated`), in whichever session the elevation runs. The action table also refuses the request and both answers to an elevation (`elevation_restricted`).
- **A late answer is refused.** After the request lifetime an answer gets `410 elevation_request_expired`, and the request is recorded as `request_expired` at that moment. A request that was answered already gets `409 elevation_request_decided`.

Each step writes its ledger row, its security audit event (`admin.elevation` with the action `requested`, `approved` or `denied`) and, for a request, its notices, in one transaction: none happens without the others.

**With approval off** (after its delay, see [Switching approval off](#switching-approval-off)), nobody is asked: the platform approves the request at once at the `admin` level, on the path `self`. Its `approved` event has the source `system` and says that no approver was involved, and no request e-mail is sent. During the delay a request still needs an approver.

**What the console reads.**

- `GET /api/v2/admin/access/elevations/mine`: the caller's latest request with its state (`requested`, `approved`, `denied` with its reason code, `request_expired`, and later the states of the elevation), the time left, and the roles that can approve it. For a holder of `access.breakglass.request`.
- `GET /api/v2/admin/access/elevations/pending`: the requests that wait for an answer, oldest first (at most 200), with their reason text and the count of the whole queue. The caller's own requests are left out. For a holder of `access.breakglass.approve`.
- `GET /api/v2/admin/access/elevations?limit=50&offset=0`: the history of requests and elevations, newest first. For a holder of `access.roles.view`. The reason text and the ticket are shown only to a holder of `access.breakglass.review`, to the person and to the approver; everyone else sees the reason code.

## When an elevation or a request runs out

Access stops by itself. An elevation grants nothing once its expiry passes, whether or not anything has run since: the permission check compares the expiry with the clock.

The record catches up on its own. The `main.elevation_reconciler` routine runs every minute (see [Schedules and tasks](/admin/schedules-and-tasks)) and, for each row, in one transaction:

- an elevation past its expiry that nobody ended is recorded as ended with the end reason `expired`, gets its `expired` security audit event, and moves to `review_pending`;
- a request that nobody approved or denied within the **request lifetime** is recorded as `request_expired` and gets its `request_expired` event. An approval of it afterwards is refused. The lifetime is the one stored when the routine runs, so lowering it also expires the pending requests older than the new value.

An elevation that ends early (by its holder, an approver or the operator command) moves to `review_pending` at the same moment, with its `ended` event. If the audit event cannot be written, the row is left as it was and the next pass completes it; access is not affected either way. One replica runs each pass, and two passes never record the same row twice.

To end an elevation without the console, see the [break-glass runbook](/deployment/break-glass-runbook).

### What else ends an elevation

An elevation is bound to one browser session and to one person, and it ends with either of them, in the same transaction:

| What happened | End reason |
|---|---|
| The person signs out of the elevated session | `logout` |
| The session is revoked (for example, all of the person's sessions are signed out) | `session_revoked` |
| The person is suspended | `user_suspended` |
| The person is deleted | `user_deleted` |
| The identity provider deactivates the person over SCIM, including a deactivation that is refused because the person is the last administrator | `scim_deactivated` |

A sign-out ends only the elevation of that session: another person's elevation, in another session, goes on. A new session of the same person does not inherit the elevation. A suspension, a deletion and a SCIM deactivation also revoke every unused sealed credential of the person, each with its `credential_revoked` event.

Each end writes its `ended` event and moves the elevation to `review_pending`. This is the one place where a failed audit write does not stop the change: a sign-out or a suspension is never undone because its audit event could not be written. The elevation already grants nothing (its session is revoked, or its person cannot sign in), and the next pass of `main.elevation_reconciler` records the end with the same reason and its event. A sealed credential whose event cannot be written is revoked without the event.

## Who is told

Break-glass is loud. Each of these events writes its notices in the same transaction as the event, and the platform then sends them by e-mail:

| Event | Who gets an e-mail |
|---|---|
| A request for an elevation | Every holder of `access.breakglass.approve`, except the person who asked. The e-mail links to the approval page. |
| An elevation starts | Every holder of the `admin` or `super_admin` role and every holder of `access.breakglass.review`. When approval is off, or the sealed credential was used, the e-mail says that no approver was involved. |
| A `critical` change made under an elevation | The same people as for a start. |
| A review that is overdue | The same people. |
| An elevation left with no eligible reviewer (`unreviewed_no_reviewer`) | The same people. |
| A review with the outcome `unauthorized` | The same people. |

The people are decided from **standing** roles when the event happens: an elevation never counts, a suspended account or one without an e-mail address is not told, and a person whose role is taken away afterwards still gets the notice of the event that happened. Nobody needs a project membership to be told.

Each e-mail names the person and the reason code. The reason text is in the e-mail only for a recipient who may read it in the console (a holder of `access.breakglass.approve` or `access.breakglass.review`), and never in a subject. The link to the approval page is built from the deployment's public address (`DEPLOYMENT_URL`), or from the public base URL of the e-mail settings when that is not set.

<Note>
Break-glass notices need outbound e-mail. Configure it (the **E-mail** section of [Configuration](/admin/features-and-configuration#configuration), or the [SMTP environment](/deployment/configuration#e-mail-smtp-bootstrap-defaults)) before you rely on break-glass. A notice is never dropped: while no mail server is configured, or while it refuses a message, the notice waits and is retried (after a minute at first, then at most every hour) by the `main.access_notice_sender` routine (see [Schedules and tasks](/admin/schedules-and-tasks#access-notice-sender)). A mail server that is down never blocks or ends an elevation. Waiting notices are rows of `elitea_auth.admin_access_notices` with no `sent_at`; the `attempts` and `last_error` columns say what was tried.
</Note>

A deployment can also send these notices to an out-of-band webhook, for when e-mail is down: see [The out-of-band notice webhook](/deployment/configuration#the-out-of-band-notice-webhook). Every event of the table but the request makes one webhook call, with ids, a type and a time only; it names nobody.

The notice in the bell icon of the application comes in a later release. The request, a critical change, an overdue review, the move to `unreviewed_no_reviewer` and an `unauthorized` review write their notices today; the start ships in a later release.

## The review after use

Every elevation is reviewed after it ends, by someone who was not involved. The review is an attestation: it changes no platform state.

**Who may review.** When an elevation ends, the platform records its reviewers: every holder of `access.breakglass.review` through a standing role (the Security auditor role by default) whose account is not suspended, except the person elevated, the requester and the approver. That record decides who may write the review, not the current roles: a reviewer whose role is taken away afterwards can still review, and a person who gets the role after the end cannot. The holder, the requester and the approver can neither read nor write the review (`403 elevation_reviewer_involved`). A personal access token, a native session, a run token and a sealed session cannot review.

**What the reviewer sees.** `GET /api/v2/admin/access/elevations/{elevationID}/review` answers the elevation with its reason, the recorded reviewers, and every administration audit row made under the elevation, flagged rows first, then by time, paged with `limit` and `offset`.

**The two outcomes.** `POST` on the same path with `outcome` and an optional `note` (at most 4000 characters) records the attestation and moves the elevation to `reviewed`:

- `justified`: the use was right.
- `unauthorized`: the use was not. The same people as an elevation start are told (see [Who is told](#who-is-told)), and a revert list of the elevation's flagged changes is opened.

A `justified` review may also confirm the roles and memberships the holder gave to other people (`confirm_grant_ids`; see [What an elevated person gives is provisional](#what-an-elevated-person-gives-is-provisional)). The review of an elevation after a sealed sign-in also says whether a replacement credential was issued (`replacement_issued`). The `admin.elevation.reviewed` security event is written in the same transaction; it carries the outcome, never the note. A second review of the same elevation answers `409 elevation_not_reviewable`.

**A reviewer cannot be removed and waited out.** While the review is pending, taking `access.breakglass.review` from one of its reviewers, suspending or deleting them needs `admin.auth.users.super_admin` (`403 elevation_pending_reviewer` otherwise). The change is listed in the review (`reviewer_changes`), and the person stays a reviewer of it.

### The review deadline

A review is due within the review time of the **Break-glass** settings: 72 hours after the elevation ended on the online path, 24 hours after a sealed sign-in. The `main.elevation_reconciler` routine checks the pending reviews on every pass:

- **A reviewer is left.** While at least one recorded reviewer still has an account that is not suspended, an overdue review stays `review_pending` and is raised **once a day**: the same people as an elevation start are told, and the webhook is called (see [Who is told](#who-is-told)). It is raised until someone reviews it.
- **Nobody is left.** When no recorded reviewer is left, at the deadline or on a later day (the last one was suspended or deleted), the elevation moves to the terminal state `unreviewed_no_reviewer`. The move is announced once: the `admin.elevation.unreviewed` security event, the notices and the webhook call. The daily reminder stops, and a review of it is refused (`409 elevation_not_reviewable`).

<Tip>
Give the Security auditor role to at least one person who will not use break-glass, so that every elevation has a reviewer. With only administrators, an elevation one administrator approved for another has nobody independent to review it, and it ends as `unreviewed_no_reviewer`.
</Tip>

### Notes and the acknowledgement

An elevation that ended as `unreviewed_no_reviewer` keeps saying so: nothing turns it into a review. Two things can still be added to its record:

- **A note from the operator.** A person with access to the workload attaches a note to any elevation, in any state, with `elitea-main break-glass note <elevation id>` (see [the break-glass runbook](/deployment/break-glass-runbook#add-a-note-to-an-elevation)). The note names no platform account.
- **The acknowledgement.** A global administrator, through a **standing** role that holds `admin.auth.users.super_admin`, acknowledges an `unreviewed_no_reviewer` elevation with a note: `POST /api/v2/admin/access/elevations/{elevationID}/acknowledge` with `{"note": "..."}`, from a browser session. It is accepted once per elevation (`409 elevation_acknowledged` after that), and only in that state (`409 elevation_not_acknowledgeable` otherwise). An elevation never supplies the permission (`403 elevation_restricted`), and a token cannot acknowledge. It records that the gap is known and who knows it; the elevation's state does not change.

The note's text stays in the elevation's record. Its `admin.elevation.note_added` security event carries the note's id and its length, never the text; an acknowledgement adds the reason `acknowledgement`.

### The revert list

An `unauthorized` review opens one entry per flagged change of the elevation. The platform reverts nothing by itself: the list is a queue for the platform administrators, the holders of `access.breakglass.manage`.

- `GET /api/v2/admin/access/reverts?open=true`: the entries not handled yet; without `open`, every entry, unhandled ones first.
- `POST /api/v2/admin/access/reverts/{itemID}/handled`: marks an entry handled, once. A Security auditor can read the review but cannot mark an entry.

Marking an entry handled writes no security audit event. The entry keeps who marked it and when (`handled_by`, `handled_at`), and that record cannot be changed afterwards.

## What an elevation cannot do

An elevation must not make itself permanent or switch off the controls that watch it. Every administration action carries an elevation value in the platform's action table, and the platform applies it when the permission that admitted the request came from the elevation:

- **allow** (most actions): the elevation may do it.
- **flag**: allowed, and the audit row of the request is marked `elevation_flagged` for the elevation review (see [Role use on administration rows](/admin/audit-trail#role-use-on-administration-rows)).
- **deny**: refused with `403` and the code `elevation_restricted`, at either level.
- **sealed**: the identity trust actions. Refused with `403 elevation_restricted` under an elevation a person approved, or one that started with approval off, at either level, and under a sealed elevation at the `admin` level. Allowed and flagged only under a sealed elevation at the `super_admin` level: repairing an identity provider while nobody can sign in is what the sealed path is for.

The value belongs to the action, not to the permission: shortening a retention policy is refused, lengthening it with the same permission is not. A person whose **standing** roles already admit an action is not restricted by these values, elevated or not. An elevation also creates no personal access token (`403 elevation_restricted` on **Create token**; a request with admin scopes gets the scope rules' own codes `token_scopes_elevated` or `token_scopes_sealed_session` first), and a sealed session never does.

### The people an elevation cannot remove

Some limits are about people, not actions, so they hold on routes the table allows:

- **The people who can stop and review the elevation stay.** An elevated session cannot demote, suspend or delete a holder of `access.breakglass.approve` or `access.breakglass.review` (`403` with the code `elevation_protected_person`). This covers both role assignment routes (the Users page and **Modes**), user suspension and user deletion. Who holds the two permissions is read from standing roles.
- **Not the own roles, not the own eligibility.** An elevated person cannot give or take any of the own roles, and so cannot change the own `access.breakglass.request`, at either level (`403 role_self_assignment`). The exemption a standing global administrator has is read from standing roles only: an elevation at the `super_admin` level never carries it.

### What an elevated person gives is provisional

An elevated person could make an elevation permanent by giving a standing role or a project membership to someone else. So what an elevated session gives to another person is **provisional**: it is recorded against the elevation, never as a standing assignment.

- **Which writes.** An administration role given on the Users page (`set_admin_role`) or on **Modes**, and a project membership given by a bulk invite, or by an invite or a role edit in a project the elevated person is not a member of. The response says so: `provisional: true` (and `provisional_role_names` for `set_admin_role`). This applies to a standing global administrator's elevated session too. A role or a membership **taken away** under an elevation is taken at once, and flagged for the review.
- **While the elevation is active.** The person holds the role or the membership, for every request, and nowhere else: one second after the elevation ends or expires it grants nothing. No job has to run. A provisional role counts as a permission that came from an elevation: the [limits above](#what-an-elevation-cannot-do) apply to its holder, and what that person gives on is provisional too. A suspended project grants nothing, provisional or not.
- **Never standing.** A provisionally granted person is never treated as holding the role through a standing role: not exempt from the role rules, not an approver, not a reviewer.
- **Where it shows.** The project's member listing returns provisional members apart from its rows (`provisional`), with the elevation that gave them. The review of the elevation lists every provisional grant (`provisional_grants`).
- **The review decides.** A `justified` review confirms the grants the reviewer names in `confirm_grant_ids`: each becomes a standing role or membership, written by the reviewer with the source `review` in the role grant history. An `unauthorized` review confirms none (a confirmation with it is `400`), and an elevation that ended as `unreviewed_no_reviewer` has none confirmed. A grant that is not confirmed stays in the record and grants nothing.

### A critical change is announced at once

A `critical` action that an elevation may do (for example changing a global provider's endpoint, or giving an administration role) is announced in the same transaction as the change: it writes the security audit event `admin.elevation.critical_change` and a notice to the same people as an elevation start (see [Who is told](#who-is-told)). If the event or the notices cannot be written, the change is rolled back. The review is not the first time someone hears of it.

Two critical actions cannot be announced this way yet, so an elevation is refused them (`403 elevation_restricted`): writing a guardrail row of the gateway governance (an elevation is refused every governance row write, because which kind of row a request writes is decided after the route), and deleting a project. A person whose standing roles admit them is not affected.

## The sealed credential list

The console reads the sealed credentials with `GET /api/v2/admin/access/sealed_credentials`, for a holder of `access.breakglass.manage`. Issuing and revoking a credential stay operator commands (see the [runbook](/deployment/break-glass-runbook#sealed-credentials)); there is no route for either.

The list is metadata only. Each row has:

- the credential's id and its custodian label;
- its level, `admin` or `super_admin`;
- the person it is bound to (id, name and e-mail address), or, for an unbound credential, the reserved break-glass account `break-glass@centry.user`, marked `unbound`;
- when it was issued, when it expires, when it was used and when it was revoked (empty when it was not), and its state at the time of the read: `active`, `used`, `revoked` or `expired`;
- whether its verifier was printed as two halves for two custodians (`split`).

It never shows the selector, the verifier, the stored digest or anything derived from them, in whole or in part. The list names the people with emergency access, so it follows the rule for lists of administrators: only a holder of `access.breakglass.manage` reads it. An elevation cannot read it (`403 elevation_restricted`, in the table above), and no personal access token can (`403 token_critical_action_refused`), whatever its scopes. Every read is recorded as a security audit decision event, `admin.elevation.credential_list`, with the number of rows, exactly as the operator command `break-glass list` records its read. That is why a client reads the list once per page view and never polls it.

A credential whose account was deleted by hand still lists, with the account's id and no name or e-mail address. If that account was the reserved one, the row reads as bound, not `unbound`, because the credential itself records only the account it names.

## Sealed sessions

A sealed session is how a [sealed credential](/deployment/break-glass-runbook#sealed-credentials) signs in when single sign-on is down. It is not an ordinary session with more permissions. It is a separate kind of session with its own limits:

- **Its own cookie.** It is carried by the cookie `__Host-elitea_sealed`, which is `Secure`, `HttpOnly` and `SameSite=Strict`, with the path `/` and no domain. It never replaces the person's ordinary session cookie, and neither cookie can stand in for the other.
- **The administration surface only.** The cookie counts only on routes of the administration surface: the administration console and the administration routes of the API. On every other route the platform ignores it, as if the browser had not sent it. A sealed session cannot open chat, agents or any project content, and it holds nothing in the developer and default modes.
- **The elevation only.** A sealed session holds the permissions of its elevation's level (`admin` or `super_admin`, the level of the credential) and nothing else. The person's standing roles add nothing: a person who is `super_admin` signs in at the `admin` level with an `admin` credential and gets only the `admin` set.
- **No identity provider.** A sealed session never refreshes, introspects or calls an identity provider. It is checked against the database only, so it works while the provider is down.
- **It ends with its elevation.** Its expiry is the elevation's expiry: the sealed duration of the settings above, and never more than 240 minutes, the ceiling in code. It also ends at once when the elevation ends early: by its holder, an approver, the operator command `break-glass end` (see the [runbook](/deployment/break-glass-runbook#end-an-elevation)), or a session or account event. From the next request on, the cookie counts as absent: the browser is told to drop it, and the request is unauthenticated unless the browser also holds an ordinary session, which then decides with the person's standing roles only.
- **The idle limit still applies.** The maximum idle time of the session policy, when one is set, applies to a sealed session too: one left unused that long is signed out before its elevation runs out. Its elevation then grants nothing (no session can use it) but stays open in the ledger until its expiry, or until `break-glass end` or the console ends it.
- **No recent-authentication proof.** An action that asks for a recent sign-in refuses a sealed session, and so does every `critical` action, which is refused under any elevation.

A sealed session needs HTTPS. Browsers accept a `__Host-` cookie only from a secure origin, so on a deployment served over plain HTTP (a local development stack, for example) the sealed path does not work. The platform does not weaken the cookie to make it work there.

<Note>
This release ships the session kind, its cookie and its limits. The sign-in page that turns a sealed credential into a sealed session comes in a later release.
</Note>
