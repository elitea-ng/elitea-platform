In the Elitea desktop app, the foot of the left sidebar shows who you are signed in as and which organization you are connected to: your picture, your name, and the deployment's name (for example **Alex K · Acme Enterprise**). Select it to open the account menu. The bug button beside it files a bug report.

## What the menu holds

| Item | What it does |
|------|--------------|
| *Your email* | The account you are signed in with. It is a label, not a link. |
| **Settings** (⌘, / Ctrl+,) | Opens Settings in a window over the app. Close it with the × button, Escape or a click outside it to return to the page you were on. |
| **Usage** | Opens Settings › Usage. It appears only when usage reporting is enabled for you. |
| **Credentials** | Opens the Credentials page. It appears only if you can see credentials. |
| **Catalog** | Opens the Elitea Catalog. |
| **Appearance** | Choose **System**, **Light** or **Dark**. The choice is saved to your account, so the web app follows it too. |
| **Language** | Choose the app's language (see [Language](#language)). The window reloads in the new language: your session, folders and threads stay, but a message you have not sent yet is lost. |
| **Get help** | Opens the Help Center ([Help and support](/menus/help-and-support)). |
| **What's new** | Opens the release notes of the version you are running, on GitHub, in your browser. The version number is shown beside it. A nightly build opens the releases list at its entry. |
| **Troubleshoot** | Opens Settings › Troubleshoot, where you can run the app's diagnostics. |
| **Sign out** | Signs this computer out of the deployment and returns to the connect screen. |

**Appearance** and **Language** open their choices beside the menu when you point at them, as menus in your operating system do; a click, or the right arrow key, opens them too. The left arrow key or Esc goes back. Every submenu in the app works this way, for example the agent's **Version** and the **Step limit**.

Credentials, Catalog and the Help Center used to be rows in the sidebar. They moved to this menu so the list holds only the places you work in. The command palette (⌘K / Ctrl+K) still finds all of them.

## The sidebar and its project

The top of the sidebar shows the **project** you are working in, under the search box. Everything below it belongs to that project. Select it to switch to another project: type part of a project's name to narrow the list, then use the arrow keys and Return, or click one. The list loads more projects as you scroll, so it stays quick when you belong to hundreds of projects.

If a thread from a folder in the current project is open when you switch, the app closes it and shows the start screen of the new project.

- **Local work** lists the folders you opened in this project. Select the **Local work** heading to open the page with all your folders. A folder you open joins the selected project. A folder that has no project yet, for example one opened before projects were assigned, shows in every project marked **No project** until you give it one.
- To move a folder to another project, use its **⋯** menu (**Move to project**) or its right-click menu (**Project**). The app asks you to confirm first. After the move, the folder shows under the other project, and its next threads use that project's models, credentials and budget. The files and past threads stay on your computer.
- Select the **Chats** heading to start a new chat, as **+** beside it does. **Chats** lists your chat folders and the date groups the app makes, such as **Today** and **Previous 7 days**. Each shows a folder icon that opens and closes. **Archived** opens your archived chats.
- **Applications** and **Artifacts** follow.
- **Workshop** groups Agents, Pipelines, Skills, Toolkits & Indexes and MCPs. Select it to open or close the group; the app remembers your choice. It is closed until you first open it, and it opens by itself when you go to one of its pages, for example from the command palette or a link.

## The message box

Chats and Local work threads share one message box. Drag its top edge to make it taller or shorter (or focus the edge and use the arrow keys; double-click resets it). What you type and do not send stays with that chat or thread until you come back.

In a Local work thread the row under the text holds, from the left:

- **+** to attach images (you can also paste or drop them).
- The mode: **Act** (edits with approvals), **Plan** (plans, changes nothing) or **Bypass** (no approvals; the app warns you the first time).
- The agent and its version (point at **Version** to see the versions), and the model.
- **⋯** with **Step limit**: how many steps a turn may take. Point at it to pick the default, a preset, **Unlimited** or a number of your own.
- In a new thread of a git folder, **Work in**: **This folder**, or **New worktree** (see [Work in a worktree](#work-in-a-worktree)).
- **Send to cloud**, or **Take back** for a thread running in the cloud (see [Send a thread to the cloud](#send-a-thread-to-the-cloud)), the language-server status, the index icon, and a ring showing how much of the model's context the thread uses. Select the ring to open the **Context** tab.
- **∞ To completion**, on by default: the turn continues by itself until the goal is met (see [Run a task to completion](#run-a-task-to-completion)).
- **Send**, which becomes **Stop** while a turn runs.

The index icon shows the state of the folder's code index by its colour: grey when it is ready or off, the accent colour while it builds, orange when it is out of date and red after an error. Point at it to read the state, for example *Index ready · 257.3K entities*; select it to open the **Index** tab on the right, where the index settings are.

While a turn runs, in a chat or a thread, that round button is **Stop** until you type, then **Send** again: what you send waits in the **Queued** list at the top of the message box. Each queued message has **Send now** (stop the turn and send this message first), **Edit** (take it back into the message box) and **Remove**. Press **Esc** twice quickly to stop the turn from anywhere on the page; a single Esc still just closes a menu or dialog.

### Run a task to completion

**∞ To completion**, just before **Send**, is on by default in each new folder. With it on, each message that you send runs to the end to reach its goal: when a turn stops at a limit, the app starts the next turn by itself. Select the chip to turn it off or on; the folder keeps your choice. Point at it to read what it does now. Select the **⌄** beside it to open its settings:

| Setting | What it does | Default |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| **Continue until the goal is met** | Answers "Continue?" at the step limit. When the turn has used its three continues, or when it reaches the cap of 512 tool calls, the app starts the next turn by itself. When it is off, the turn stops at a limit and waits for you. | On |
| **Change limits** | At most this many automatic continuations, this spend in US dollars and this many hours. Empty fields use the platform's defaults (20 continuations, 10 × the project's per-run spend cap, 12 hours). Your administrator sets the maximums. If a value is too high, the platform refuses the grant and the app tells you which limit to lower. | Platform defaults |
| **Check the goal before finishing** | A small model writes a brief of your goal and checks it before the run ends. When a check is not met, the app starts the next turn by itself. When it is off, the run ends when the agent stops. See [The brief and the goal check](#the-brief-and-the-goal-check). The folder keeps your choice. | On |
| **Hook asks** | A `PreToolUse` hook that asks for your approval: the call runs. | Off |
| **Remote confirmations** | A toolkit that asks you to confirm a call: the call runs. | Off |

When **Hook asks** or **Remote confirmations** is on, the chip has an orange outline: the app also answers for you.

- **To completion** does not change the mode. Your approval rules still ask in **Act** mode; only **Bypass** mode runs the calls without the question. The policy, every deny rule, the sandbox and the budgets always apply.
- **To completion** is not used in **Plan** mode: the plan waits for you.
- A question that the settings do not answer waits for you, as it does in a turn that you watch. When the wait runs out, the question takes its default.
- An MCP sign-in is not answered for you. Sign in to the MCP servers before you start a long task.
- Each turn the app starts by itself shows **Continued automatically** at its top. The message box stays busy between the turns. The last turn shows how the run ended: completed, failed, waiting for you, stopped at a chain limit (and which one), or cancelled. After a turn is saved, the next turn must start within a minute; if it does not, the run ends as cancelled. A slow save does not end the run.
- If your administrator turns unattended grants off, the app does not continue a run for you, and a run that is in progress stops at its next continuation. When the platform refuses a grant for this reason, the chip goes away until you open the app again. A grant with **Hook asks** or **Remote confirmations** shows the refusal; send the message again without them.
- Every answer is saved with the thread, with who gave it: you, the grant, bypass mode, or nobody (the wait ran out).

Questions have a time limit: an approval or a hook ask waits 30 minutes, a toolkit confirmation 15 minutes and "Continue?" 10 minutes. After that the call is declined, or the turn stops.

#### The brief and the goal check

When **Check the goal before finishing** is on, a small model reads your message and writes a brief. The brief has the goal, up to five checks ("Done when"), the constraints and the assumptions. The first turn does not wait for the brief.

- The first answer shows the brief as a card: **Goal · Done when: 3 checks**. Select the arrow on the card to see the goal, the checks, the constraints and the assumptions.
- While the model writes the brief, the card shows **Writing the brief…**. If the model cannot write the brief, no card shows, and the run ends as before.
- A model wrote the brief from your message. When they do not agree, your message wins. The brief never adds a tool, an approval or a grant.
- A message with nothing to check, such as a greeting or a short question, gets a goal and no checks. The card then shows the goal only, and the run ends as before.
- Before the run ends, the model compares the run's transcript with the checks. The answer that the model checked shows the result:
  - **Done · 3 of 3 checks · model-reviewed**: all checks are met. The run ends as completed.
  - **Not done yet: …**: a check is not met. The app starts the next turn by itself with the reason. This continuation counts against the chain limits.
  - **Blocked on: …**: a check cannot be met, or the same check is not met three times in a row. The run ends as failed.
  - **Not checked**: the check did not answer in time. The run ends as completed.
- The brief and the check use the project's low-tier model. Their cost counts against the spend limit of the run.
- Your administrator can turn the brief and the check off for all users. The run then ends as before.

#### Long runs: sleep, closing and restarts

- While a turn runs, the computer does not go to sleep when it is idle. The menu of the menu-bar icon (the tray icon on Windows and Linux) shows **Keeping the computer awake**. When the last turn ends, the computer can sleep again. The screen can still turn off, and you can still put the computer to sleep.
- On Windows, when you close the window while a turn runs, the app hides the window and the turn continues. Select the tray icon to show the window again.
- On Linux, when you close the window while a turn runs, the app minimizes the window and the turn continues. Many Linux desktops do not show a tray icon. Select the app in the task bar or the dock to show the window again.
- On Windows and Linux, when no turn runs, closing the window closes the app. On macOS, closing the window always hides it.
- When you quit the app while a turn runs, the app asks first. Select **Quit** to stop the turn and quit, or **Keep running** to cancel. When the app restarts to install an update, it does not ask.
- When the app stops during a run with **To completion** (for example, the computer restarts), the app continues the run when you open it again. The next turn shows **Resumed after restart** at its top. It continues from the last saved turn, with the brief and the last goal check. The chain limits still apply: a run that is past its time limit does not continue.
- After a restart, the goal check reads the turns from before the restart and the turns after it.
- A turn without **To completion** does not continue by itself. After a restart, the turn shows **This turn did not finish** and a **Continue** button. Select **Continue** to start the next turn.
- When the platform refuses to continue the run (for example, at a chain limit), the run ends, and the last turn shows why.
- When the app cannot continue the run yet (for example, you are not signed in, or the network is down), it tries again later: after each sign-in, and at most every 5 minutes. You can also select **Continue**.

### Send a thread to the cloud

A thread that may move to the cloud shows **Send to cloud** in the row under the text. Select it to review what is sent and send the thread. The small **⌄** beside it opens **Handoff settings…**, where you choose what stays on your computer.

While the thread runs in the cloud, the same place shows **Take back**: select it to review the cloud's changes and apply them to your folder. Its **⌄** holds **Erase cloud copy…**, **Open in chat** and **Handoff settings…**. While the thread is on its way back it shows **Returning** and cannot be selected.

When a thread cannot move (your deployment or project has the cloud executor off, or the thread has no messages yet), nothing is shown: the thread runs on your computer.

#### Send a running task and continue in the cloud

You can send a thread while its turn is still running, for example before you close your laptop:

- The dialog says that a turn is running. When you select **Send to cloud**, the turn stops at its next step, so nothing it has done is lost. The turn is then saved, and the platform sends the thread and starts the cloud's first turn together: either both happen, or neither does. The cloud continues with the same mode.
- When the thread runs with **To completion**, the cloud continues the same run. The chain limits (continuations, spend and hours) count across your computer and the cloud. When the run ends, the notification says it ended in the cloud.
- If the platform does not allow the run to continue in the cloud (for example, a chain limit is reached, or your administrator turned unattended grants off), the thread is still sent. The dialog says why, and the thread waits in the cloud for your next message. Select **OK** to close the dialog.
- A thread in **Plan** mode is sent without continuing: the cloud has no plan mode, so the turn stops, the thread is sent, and it waits in the cloud for your next message.
- The send checks for secrets, files to confirm, environment variables and size limits before it stops the turn. If one of these checks fails, the turn keeps running.
- If the send fails or you cancel it after the turn stopped, the run goes on on your computer at once. If it cannot go on, a notice tells you why.
- A turn that is waiting for your answer (an approval, or **Continue?** at its step limit) stops only after you answer it, or when the question times out.
- The cloud runs the turn as any cloud turn. Plan mode, hook asks and remote confirmations are parts of the desktop app and do not apply in the cloud.

**Take back when done** brings the thread back to this computer by itself when its cloud run completes. It is on by default when a turn is running, and you can turn it on or off in the dialog before you send. A thread sent this way shows **Takes back when done** above the text box. The platform keeps your choice and the result of the last cloud run, but only your desktop app takes the thread back. When the run completes, the app takes the thread back even after a restart. It also checks when you sign in, and every few minutes. You do not need to keep the thread open:

- If your folder did not change, or the changes do not overlap, the cloud's changes are applied to your folder. A notice says **Took back** and the thread's name.
- If your folder and the cloud changed the same lines, the cloud's version opens as a separate worktree, and your folder is not changed. A notice says **Opened … as a worktree**.
- If the run failed, waits for you, or stopped at its limit, the thread stays in the cloud. A notice tells you once, so that you can look at it and take it back yourself.

### The Changes tab

The **Changes** tab on the right lists what each turn changed, newest first. Each turn is one line: the start of your message, the number of files and the lines added and removed. Select it to fold or unfold the turn; the newest turn starts unfolded. Point at a turn for **Undo** (or **Restore folder** after an undo).

Each file is one row: its name, its folder in grey, the lines added and removed and a coloured letter, **M** modified, **A** added, **D** deleted or **R** renamed. Select a row to open its diff below it. Point at the row, or move to it with the keyboard, for **Reveal in Finder**, **Open** and **Revert**.

### The thread's title

The thread's title at the top (folder › thread) opens a card with the folder's path, **Reveal in Finder** and **Copy Path**. To move the folder to another project, use the folder's menus in the sidebar.

Until you choose one, the app follows your operating system's preferred languages and picks the closest one it has. For example, Canadian French opens in Français (Canada), Belgian French in Français, and Hong Kong Chinese in 繁體中文. If none of your system's languages is available, the app opens in English.

Arabic, Hebrew, Persian and Urdu are written right to left. In those languages the whole window is mirrored: the sidebar is on the right, and arrows and disclosure chevrons point the other way. File names, paths, code and product names keep their own direction.

The app's menu bar (File, Edit, View…) and the menu-bar icon's menu follow the same language.

Your choice applies to this computer only. Signing out clears it, so the next person to sign in on a shared computer gets their system's language.

Strings that have no translation yet are shown in English. Names of products and technical terms, such as MCP, DeepWiki or GitHub, are not translated. The translations are new, so if a label reads wrong, report it with the bug button.

## Work in a worktree

A worktree is a separate copy of a git folder on its own branch. The agent works there while you keep using the folder, or while another thread works in it, so several tasks run side by side without touching each other's files.

A new thread in a git folder starts with **Work in** set to **New worktree**. Write your message and send it: the app makes the worktree on a new `elitea/…` branch from the folder's current commit, opens the worktree's thread and sends the message there. To work in the folder itself, set **Work in** to **This folder** before you send.

The app remembers your choice for each folder: after you pick **This folder**, the folder's next new thread starts on **This folder** too, until you pick **New worktree** again. **New thread in a worktree** in the folder's **⋯** or right-click menu always starts on **New worktree**.

A worktree shows under its folder in the sidebar, as one of the folder's threads with a branch icon, the thread's title, and how many commits it is ahead and how many files are not committed. Hover over the name to see the branch and where the worktree is. Hover actions:

- **Diff** shows what the task changed.
- **Merge back** previews the merge into the folder, then applies it as uncommitted changes. You can undo it.
- **Remove** deletes the worktree; the app asks first when it has uncommitted work. Its branch stays when it has commits.

A worktree is never listed as a folder of its own, on the **Local work** page or in the command palette.

## Archive and delete threads

Each thread under **Local work** has **Archive** and **Delete…** in its **⋯** menu (hover over the thread) and in its right-click menu. They work the same for a thread that runs on this computer and one you sent to the cloud.

- **Archive** archives the thread's conversation, as archiving a chat does. The thread leaves **Local work** and shows under **Archived** in the **Chats** section. Unarchive it there and it shows under its folder again.
- **Delete…** asks first, then deletes the conversation and the thread's history on this computer. It can't be undone.

When the thread works in a worktree, archiving or deleting it also removes the worktree. The app asks first, with **Also remove its worktree** checked; clear it to keep the worktree. The worktree's `elitea/…` branch stays when it has commits. When the worktree has changes that were never committed, the app asks again: **Remove anyway** deletes those changes, **Keep the worktree** leaves it in the sidebar.

Unarchiving a thread whose worktree was removed restores its conversation, but the thread does not show under **Local work** again: its worktree is gone.

If the thread you archive or delete is open, the app opens a new thread in its folder.

## When the app cannot reach your deployment

At launch, or after your computer wakes, the network may not be up yet. The app keeps asking your deployment who you are, after 1, 2, 4 and 8 seconds and then every 15 seconds, and opens your work as soon as it answers. You don't need to restart it. If it stays on "Loading…", check your connection or VPN.

## Report a bug

The bug button opens a new issue in the public [elitea-ng/elitea-platform](https://github.com/elitea-ng/elitea-platform/issues) repository in your browser. The issue is prefilled with headings for what happened, what you expected and the steps to reproduce it, plus your app version, platform and language. Review it before you submit it. You need a GitHub account.

<Note>
The repository is public. The report never includes your deployment's address, your organization or your identity, and you should not add them, or any tokens or customer data. If logs would help, Settings › Troubleshoot shows them.
</Note>
