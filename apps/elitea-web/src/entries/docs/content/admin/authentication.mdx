Authentication lives under **Configuration › Authentication** in the admin console. It covers how people sign in (OIDC or SAML) and how an identity provider's groups map onto project membership (SCIM).

## Configure an identity provider

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Configuration › Authentication">
    The list shows any providers already configured, one live per protocol.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a provider">
    Choose **OpenID Connect** or **SAML 2.0**.

    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="OpenID Connect">
        Issuer URL (a `.well-known/openid-configuration` URL is accepted and reduced to its issuer), Client ID, client secret, redirect URI, scopes, and whether e-mail verification is required.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="SAML 2.0">
        Identity-provider entity ID, SSO URL, single-logout URL, and certificate(s); this service's own entity ID and ACS URL; name-ID format; the e-mail and name attribute mappings; whether to sign AuthnRequests; the service provider certificate; and clock-skew tolerance.
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide whether it may sign in SCIM-provisioned users">
    **Users provisioned by SCIM may sign in through this provider** is off by default. Turn it on only for the provider paired with your SCIM client (for example, Entra ID's sign-in app when Entra also provisions users). While it is off, a first sign-in through this provider cannot take over an account that SCIM created; the sign-in is refused as a conflict. A provider configured through environment variables instead of this screen uses `OIDC_ADOPT_SCIM_USERS=true` for the same setting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable it for logins">
    Turning a provider on retires any other provider of the same protocol — only one live OIDC and one live SAML definition can exist at a time, because each protocol is served on one route.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot id="admin-auth-provider" alt="Authentication section listing configured identity providers" mask={["client-secret-field", "certificate-field"]} />

<Note>
A stored secret is never shown in the clear — the field renders a mask, and there is no "reveal" control. To change it, enter a new value; leaving it blank keeps the existing one.
</Note>

<Warning>
Deleting a provider also deletes its stored secret, which cannot be recovered. To stop using a provider for new logins without losing its configuration, turn off "use this provider for logins" instead of deleting it.
</Warning>

## Bind SCIM groups to a project role

SCIM lets an identity provider push its directory — creating and deactivating accounts as people join and leave — rather than only federating logins. The SCIM endpoint is `/api/v2/scim/v2`, authenticated with a personal access token; configuring it in your identity provider is outside Elitea's UI.

What the admin console does provide is the group-to-role mapping, beneath the identity provider list:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a binding">
    Name the SCIM group exactly as your identity provider sends it, and choose the project and role it grants.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Understand membership">
    A member the group granted the role to loses it when they leave the group. A member who already held that role independently keeps it — the identity provider is authoritative only over what it granted, not over everything it happens to find.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
A SCIM group with no binding is refused by name; it never creates a project on its own, and it never guesses a role.
</Note>

## What SCIM may and may not change

SCIM manages ordinary accounts: it creates them, renames them, changes their display name and name parts, and deactivates them. A SCIM `DELETE` deactivates (suspends) the account instead of deleting it, so the person's work stays attached if they return.

Some accounts are off limits to SCIM. Any write to them is refused with `403` (`scimType: mutability`), the reason is included in the response, and the refused attempt is recorded in the **Audit Trail**:

- **Accounts with an administration role** (any role on the admin console's Users page). SCIM cannot rename, suspend or deprovision them, including people who became administrators after SCIM created their account. This stops a SCIM credential from taking over or locking out the people who administer the platform.
- **Platform accounts** — addresses in the `@centry.user` domain, such as `system@centry.user` and the per-project `system_user_<n>@centry.user`. SCIM also cannot give any account an address in that domain, and a SCIM group cannot grant a project role to a platform account.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Deprovision an administrator">
    When your identity provider tries to deprovision someone who holds an administration role, the request is refused with "SCIM deprovisioning refused: account holds an administration role — remove the role, then let the IdP retry". Until then, the person's sessions and access tokens keep working.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove the role">
    On the admin console's **Users** page, remove the person's administration role. You can also suspend them from that page right away.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let the identity provider retry">
    The next provisioning cycle repeats the request, and this time it succeeds.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
SCIM stores one address per account, and that address is the SCIM `userName`. A change to the work email alone is accepted but does not change the account's address. Only a `userName` change does.
</Note>
